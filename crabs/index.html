01 / Study

How to Use Crabs

Learn first, retain with Anki

Crabs is an otolaryngology review resource for medical students and residents. Use an appropriate learning source to understand a topic, then find and unsuspend the corresponding Crabs cards for ongoing review. The cards support retention; they do not replace learning from the source material.

Find the material you have studied

In Anki’s Browse window, use the tag sidebar to find a topic or source, inspect the cards, and unsuspend the cards that correspond to material you have learned. Expand a branch to narrow your selection. A note can generate several cards and carry several tags, so the same material may appear under multiple branches.

The explorer below shows the actual exported tag names. Counts describe the package, not your personal study progress. A branch’s note count includes descendants, with each note counted once within that branch. Do not add counts across branches.

Use the organizational structure

The historical deck index describes organization by learning stage, source, specialty, procedure, and anatomy. These paths are present in the reviewed package:

  • Crabs::elective and Crabs::Sub-I: the historical rotation groupings. Use the current lowercase spelling of elective when locating it in the explorer.
  • Crabs::Specialty: specialty organization, including HN, FacialPlastics, Peds, Otology, OtologyNeurotology, Rhinology, Laryngology, General, Sleep, and Trauma.
  • Crabs::secrets_ent, Crabs::Pasha, and Crabs::AAOHNS_PrimaryCareOtolaryngology: resource-based organization described in the historical index.
  • Crabs::Procedures: procedure-related organization. Read the relevant learning material before reviewing associated cards. Inspect the notes for their references.
  • Crabs::extra_anatomy: additional anatomy organization; notes may also belong to specialty or procedure branches.
  • Crabs::Pimped: historically described by the author as questions encountered during rotations.
  • Crabs::Residency: a separate branch with its own topic hierarchy.
  • Crabs::Index and Crabs::Resources: index and resource notes described by the historical write-up.

Tags are an organizational aid, not an independently validated curriculum or a promise of complete coverage. Some tags are legacy, abbreviated, or inconsistently spelled. The explorer preserves them so you can see what is actually present.

Searching in Anki

Select tags in Browse to avoid copying outdated examples. For a simple current example, tag:Crabs::Sub-I searches that tag and its subtags. Anki’s search syntax has special characters; copying a path containing *, quotes, or other search operators may require escaping. The website search is a literal substring filter, not an Anki query generator.

See the Anki searching manual for combinations and escaping. The original index’s filtered-deck recipes have not been carried forward as general study instructions; their intended use needs author review.
